# Paper Writing Rules — IEEE TNSRE

## Journal Rules
IEEE TRANSACTIONS ON NEURAL SYSTEMS AND REHABILITATION ENGINEERING (TNSRE) focuses on the rehabilitative and neural aspects of biomedical engineering, including functional electrical stimulation, acoustic dynamics, human performance measurement and analysis, nerve stimulation, electromyography, motor control and stimulation, and hardware and software applications for rehabilitation engineering and assistive devices.

Authors are encouraged to submit manuscripts as regular full-length papers reporting substantially novel methodology, technology, experimentation and high-quality original results. To qualify for publication, these must be previously unpublished and not be under consideration elsewhere. The Editor-in-Chief and the Associate Editor in charge will perform a quick review of the manuscript to determine the suitability of the paper for consideration by TNSRE based on its topical relevance, quality, and availability of expertise on the editorial board, and papers that are determined to be unsuitable will be returned without further review. Manuscripts will ONLY be accepted in electronic format through the IEEE Author Portal.

Manuscripts
For purposes of qualifying for peer review and consideration for publication, authors must submit a properly executed copyright form in favor of IEEE, sign an author consent form, and prepare their manuscript according to the following guidelines. Submitted manuscripts must be:

Formatted in the transactions template: either Word or LaTeX 
10 pages or less, not counting references. Manuscripts that exceed 10 pages in the template will not be admitted for review.
Review articles can be up to 12 pages, not counting references. Review articles must have the editor-in-chief’s permission before they are submitted. Authors interested in submitting a review paper must send an email proposal and the cv of the primary author(s) to the editor-in-chief. See the TNSRE Review Paper Submission Requirement PDF and the points below for details.
How does your research topic fit within the scope of IEEE TNSRE? Briefly discuss the importance of your review to the IEEE TNSRE community.
Is there a recent review (within the past 5 years) on this topic? If so, explain how your review differs from and adds value compared to existing reviews.
How many papers are included in your review?
What are the main takeaways or conclusions of your review?
Have the authors conducted prior research on the review topic? If applicable, provide a list of related publications of yours.Once you have received permission, please include the email confirmation from the EiC or Deputy EiC as your cover letter when submitting your review paper.
Manuscript text must to be 10pt or larger for the main text, and 8pt or larger for tables and figures.
Since January 1, 2018 EMBS journals no longer publish author bios or photos.
Abstract can be no more than 250 words for regular papers.
Figures and tables should be interspersed within the paper, numbered sequentially, with features clearly marked and detailed legends. High-resolution figures may be uploaded separately if needed.
References should appear in a separate reference section at the end of the text body with items referred to by numerals in square brackets (IEEE format). See the template for details.
Research involving human or animal subjects must include a statement in the Methods Section that the research was performed under the oversight of an institutional review board or ethics committee, including the official name of the IRB/ethics committee and also including the number of the protocol. Research involving human subjects must include a statement that informed consent was received from all human subjects. If the paper reports a clinical trial, the paper needs to state the registered clinical trial number.
All photos of people must be either anonymized or a statement must be included with the submission that the person depicted agreed to the use of their image.
The Editorial Board encourages authors who have published work in peer-reviewed conference proceedings to consider expanding their work and submitting a manuscript for consideration to TNSRE. The original idea or concept must have been substantially expanded (further experiments, a greater number of subjects tested, more in-depth analysis, in-clinic testing, etc.). A good rule-of-thumb is 40% new content. Upon submission to TNSRE, the authors should inform the Editor-in-Chief about all related submissions that may be regarded as significantly overlapping or duplicate publications. The author should explain the overlap, as well as the novelty of the journal submission. The conference article should be correctly cited in the author’s Cover Letter and a copy should be uploaded with the submission so that it can be inspected alongside the journal submission.

Title and Abstract
During the login of an electronic submission in ScholarOne Manuscripts, the corresponding author will be asked to enter: the title, names and contact information for all authors (full mailing address, institutional affiliations, phone, and e-mail), and the abstract. The name of the Corresponding Author who will serve as the main point of contact for the manuscript during the review and publication processes is entered separately. The Abstract should be no longer than 250 words, indicate the scope of the paper, and summarize the authors’ conclusions. This will make the abstract, by itself, a useful tool for information retrieval.

Illustrations and Tables
For review, illustrations/tables should be interspersed within the manuscript. However, due to production requirements, for the final submission, illustrations/tables will be submitted separately as well. Each figure and table should have a caption that is intelligible without requiring reference to the text.

Electronic Guidelines
Authors are requested to submit finalized manuscripts using the style files established by IEEE. Authors should follow the instructions and read the information in the IEEE Author Center.

Suggested Reviewers and Conflicts of Interest
TNSRE asks authors to suggest qualified reviewers for their manuscripts. There is no guarantee that the editors will use the suggested reviewers.

To ensure that all manuscript reviews are fair, objective, and free from bias, do not suggest reviewers with whom you may have a conflict of interest.
For TNSRE, the following situations are considered conflicts of interest:

1. Institutional Affiliation:
You are employed by, or hold a significant professional role within the same organization as one or more of the authors. In multi-institutional organizations, this applies if you and any of the authors are part of the same division, department, or organizational unit.

2. Recent or Ongoing Collaboration:
You are currently collaborating, planning to collaborate, or have collaborated in the past three years with any author on the manuscript. This includes:

Co-authorship of papers
Joint participation in grant proposals or funded projects
Involvement in shared research initiatives or significant academic partnerships
3. Mentorship Relationships:
You have had a mentoring or supervisory relationship with any of the authors – for example, as a thesis advisor, postdoctoral mentor, student, or mentee. If an author was your direct mentor or mentee, a minimum of five years should have passed since the conclusion of that training relationship.

4. Personal or Financial Relationships:

You have a close personal, romantic, familial, or financial relationship with any of the authors.

You stand to gain financially from the outcome of the manuscript’s review.

If you are uncertain whether a particular situation constitutes a conflict of interest, do not suggest the reviewer.

## Hard constraints (build fails if violated)
1. Every \cite{} key must exist in refs.bib — run scripts/check_cites.py before committing
2. Every numerical result must trace to a row in claims.csv with evidence_file pointing to results/*.json or results/*.log
3. No BibTeX entry may be hand-written — all entries come from CrossRef/OpenAlex via scripts/build_bib.py
4. Every cite must be verified to actually support its sentence — unverified cites go in scripts/to-verify.txt

## Style rules  
5. Write Methods before Results, Results before Related Work, Related Work before Introduction
6. Each paragraph in a section must correspond to a claims.csv entry before prose is drafted
7. No sentence asserts X "outperforms" Y without citing a specific metric+significance test
8. Statistical reporting format: mean ± std (p=X, Wilcoxon signed-rank, N=Y folds)
9. The two-model framing (within-dataset: nrde K=3 / cross-dataset: SpecSPD-DANN) must be explained with explicit motivation in Methods §1

## AI-ism watchlist (flag and fix before submission)
- "delve", "leverage" (as verb), "it is worth noting", "moreover" overuse, "in conclusion"
- Uniform sentence length — vary short declarative with longer analytical
- Passive-voice-only paragraphs — mix with active
